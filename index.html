Programmable modular structures that selectively disassemble using a single remote vibration source

Selectively disassembling large modular structures normally requires human intervention, robotic manipulation, or actuators and electronics distributed throughout the system.

We developed a load-bearing modular system that disassembles without onboard electronics. A single vibration source transmits a mechanical signal through the structure, while connector geometry determines release threshold and separation behavior.

Turning a standard 3D printer into a mechanical testing platform

Mechanical properties of 3D-printed parts are normally measured after fabrication using dedicated testing equipment, separating material qualification from manufacturing.

We developed a printer-integrated platform that uses print-head motion and camera-based tracking to recover modulus, damping, post-yield response, and fracture strain from a single printed cantilever. The reconstructed properties were validated against ASTM D790 testing and used to predict functional structure performance.

Ultralight mechanical metamaterials for subhertz vibration isolation

Isolating ultralow-frequency vibration normally requires large resonating masses, creating a conflict between vibration performance and structural weight.

We experimentally validated an inerter-based metamaterial that converts translational motion into amplified rotational inertia. The system produced longitudinal and transverse band gaps at 0.4–1.0 Hz and 4.6–6.2 Hz while reducing the required embedded mass by 99.9%. I contributed to the transmission experiments and led experimental data analysis and visualization.

Passive attachment and release systems for marine mammal tags

Marine mammal tags must remain securely attached long enough to collect behavioral and acoustic data, but they must also release safely at the end of deployment.

For Project CETI, we developed a pressure-driven vacuum regeneration system that uses changing hydrostatic pressure during whale dives to restore suction without battery power. I built and tested early syringe-based prototypes at pressures up to 900 psi, compared system configurations and chamber volumes, integrated pressure sensing, and developed compact burn-wire release concepts.
