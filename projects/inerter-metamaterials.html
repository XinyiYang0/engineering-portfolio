Ultralight Inerter-Based Metamaterials for Subhertz Vibration Isolation

Ultralow-frequency vibration isolation normally requires large resonating masses. We experimentally realized an inerter-based mechanical metamaterial that converts small translational motion into amplified rotational inertia, producing subhertz vibration attenuation with minimal added mass.

01

The Engineering Challenge

Low-frequency vibrations can reduce the durability, stability, and performance of buildings, vehicles, aerospace systems, and mechanical equipment.

Conventional locally resonant metamaterials create low-frequency band gaps by adding resonating masses. Moving the band gap toward lower frequencies usually requires either a much heavier resonator or an extremely soft connection. Both approaches are difficult to implement in lightweight engineering structures.

Can a lightweight mechanical system generate the dynamic effect of a much larger mass and block ultralow-frequency vibration?

02

Translation-Rotation Inertia Amplification

An inerter is a two-terminal mechanical device that converts relative translational motion between its terminals into flywheel rotation.

By using a small input radius and a larger rotational radius, a small translational displacement can produce rapid flywheel rotation. The resulting rotational inertia behaves like a much larger effective translational mass.

m2 Physical inerter mass

The actual mass added to each metamaterial unit cell.

α Inertia amplification coefficient

Determined by the geometry and transmission ratio of the inerter mechanism.

αm2 Amplified effective inertia

The equivalent dynamic inertia generated by the rotating mechanism.

fL Lower band-gap frequency

Increasing inertia amplification shifts vibration attenuation toward lower frequencies.

Translation → Gear or shear coupling → Flywheel rotation → Wave attenuation

Two Experimental Platforms

We developed two seven-unit experimental platforms to test whether the same inerter principle could attenuate both longitudinal and transverse waves.

Shear Inerter

Transverse vibration attenuation

  • Acrylic and 3D-printed components
  • Hinges, bearings, and rotating flywheels

03

Building-Scale Extension

A 7 × 7 two-dimensional array was simulated to evaluate the design at a structure-relevant scale.

Array size: 3.5 m × 3.5 m Predicted gap: approximately 1 Hz

The intact and defective systems retained similar attenuation behavior, while the empty control frame showed no band gap. This confirmed that the effect originated from the embedded inerters.

Engineering Outcomes and Tools

Engineering Outcomes

  • Experimentally demonstrated a subhertz longitudinal vibration band gap.
  • Reduced the mass required for equivalent low-frequency attenuation by 99.9%.
  • Validated the inerter concept for both longitudinal and transverse waves.
  • Converted translational vibration into localized flywheel rotation.
  • Demonstrated attenuation robustness after removing multiple inerter units.
  • Extended the design to a two-dimensional, building-scale metamaterial array.
